For the research presented in this paper a kidney belt with thermal actuators was developed in order to investigate the emotional responses to thermally augmented audiovisual stimuli (movie clips). Participants then continuously evaluated their emotional state via a tablet application on the dimensions “arousal” and “valence”. We examine whether adding controlled thermal cues to audiovisual content modulates emotional responses. In a within-subjects experiment using affectively charged video clips, synchronized warm and cool stimuli intensified valence and arousal ratings and altered engagement without harming perceived quality. Findings suggest thermal augmentation as a promising channel for affective XR and multisensory media.
