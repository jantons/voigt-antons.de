Medium — for a session page · 90 words

Jan-Niklas Voigt-Antons is Professor of Computer Science (Immersive Media) at Hamm-Lippstadt University of Applied Sciences and Director of the Immersive Reality Lab. Trained as a psychologist and as an engineer, he pairs EEG, eye tracking and physiological sensing with extended reality deployed in hospitals, public space and safety-critical training — measuring what questionnaires cannot capture. He co-developed ITU-T Recommendation P.812 on subjective test methods for interactive virtual reality, has led more than twenty funded projects, and was previously a senior research scientist at TU Berlin and at Telekom Innovation Laboratories.

Long — for a press release · 198 words

Jan-Niklas Voigt-Antons is Professor of Computer Science (Immersive Media) at Hamm-Lippstadt University of Applied Sciences, where he directs the Immersive Reality Lab, and a guest researcher at TU Berlin. His route into the field was unusual: vocational training in data technology, then a Diplom in psychology, then a doctorate in engineering. That combination defines the work. He asks how immersive and interactive systems can be designed and evaluated so that they improve experience quality, task performance and trust — and answers it by pairing EEG, eye tracking and physiological sensing with systems deployed outside the laboratory, in hospitals, in public space and in safety-critical training. His five research lines run from spatial interaction and Quality of Experience through psychophysiological measurement to digital health, learning and generative AI in media contexts. He co-developed ITU-T Recommendation P.812 on subjective test methods for interactive virtual reality and has contributed to standardization at ITU-T, MPEG and DIN. He has raised third-party funding continuously since 2015, supervises doctoral researchers in projects of their own, and is regularly asked to speak outside computer science — by medical societies, care providers, public administrations and, most recently, a United Kingdom government foresight programme on immersive technologies.
